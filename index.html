Planned size & build cost — set per property, not one flat guess

The old model priced every lot as the same 4,500 sq ft build at $275/sq ft. That misprices both ends: it overstates what a small or tightly-zoned lot can carry, and understates the cost of the luxury product these streets actually sell. The model now sets two inputs per property before the spread math runs:

Planned size = the lesser of two numbers

1. What zoning allows on that lot (living sq ft, garage taken out of the cap):

Metro Nashville has no floor-area cap — size is capped by lot coverage × 2 stories, with the garage inside the footprint (Metro Code Table 17.12.020A): RS40/R40 25% · RS30 30% · RS20/R20 35% · RS15/R15 35% · RS10/R10 40% · R8/RS7.5 45% · R6/RS5 50%. Height is separately capped at 2.5 stories / 35 ft in most districts under BL2025-1006 (RS40/R40 keep 3 stories), and a contextual front setback — in Green Hills routinely 50–100+ ft — can bind before coverage does.

City of Belle Meade (county zoning code 3ZZ) has a real floor-area ratio: 0.225 × lot on lots ≤ 40,000 sq ft, 0.20 above, gross floor area capped at 20,000 sq ft including garages, footprint ≤ 12% of lot (8% in Estates A) — Ordinance 2024-14, §14-205.

City of Forest Hills (5ZZ) sets FAR by district from 0.075 (E1A) to 0.180 (NC1/NC2), primary structure capped at 25,000 sq ft — Ordinance 2024-03, Table 4.02. County records don't carry the parcel's Forest Hills district, so these lots are modeled at a 0.13 central FAR.

City of Oak Hill (8ZZ) is modeled on its Zone C standard: gross floor area ≤ 0.18 × lot, capped at 10,000 sq ft. ⚠️ That figure rests on a single secondary summary of the city's posted Bulk Building Standards — the codified per-zone tables could not be verified — so treat Oak Hill sizes as the model's softest input and confirm with the city before underwriting.

2. What builders demonstrably build — from the 20 assessor-confirmed new builds among this page's 85 verified $2M+ new-build sales. Finished size barely scales with lot size: lots ≤ 25,000 sq ft → 5,350 sq ft median · 25,000–43,560 sq ft → 6,012 sq ft · over an acre → 5,200 sq ft. Median new build overall: 5,861 sq ft, selling at a median ~$695 per finished sq ft. Bigger lots mostly buy yard, not house — so the market number, not the zoning maximum, is what usually binds.

HPR two-unit play: where a lot is in a Metro R district (never RS) and ≥ 15,000 sq ft, the model prices two homes instead of one: exit = 2 × the per-side exit from actual HPR-side sales (the 11 A/B sales on this page run $2.0M–$3.05M per side), planned size = 2 × 4,700 sq ft (observed Green Hills HPR production runs 4,383–5,447 sq ft of living area per unit), capped by the same coverage rule with two garages. 443 of the 5,699 modeled properties qualify; 64 survive the offer screen.

Build cost = the jurisdiction's tier, plus demolition

$350/sq ft in Belle Meade, Forest Hills, and Oak Hill · $315/sq ft in Metro Nashville — hard cost per finished sq ft for the luxury spec product that sells at $2M–$5M, excluding land, plus a flat $20,000 demolition allowance per property. Soft costs (8% of exit) and builder profit (15% of exit) are unchanged.

Where the tiers come from: the recommended modeling range for Nashville luxury spec builds is $325–$425/sq ft (central ≈ $350) — NAHB's 2024 cost survey puts average construction cost at ~$162/sq ft nationally (production and custom mixed), Nashville custom builders publish $200–$350+/sq ft with luxury at $400+, and one real Brentwood-area lender package prices at $255.90/sq ft vertical plus ~$77/sq ft of site and program cost — ≈ $333/sq ft all-in. The Metro tier sits just under the band because Green Hills / West Meade product skews to the band's lower half; the satellite-city tier sits at the band's center. Aggregator sites (HomeBlue, Houzeo) publish far lower Nashville figures ($120–$210/sq ft) — they describe builder-grade product, not this segment, and are not used.

Demolition: HomeBlue's Nashville guide (aggregator) puts a house teardown at $8,800–$18,500 depending on size and basement, before permits, utility disconnects, and asbestos survey/abatement on pre-1985 houses — the recommended all-in modeling figure is $15,000–$30,000; the model uses $20,000.

Nashville-specific risks the flat allowance can't see: shallow limestone shelf rock across the Belle Meade / Oak Hill / Forest Hills hills (excavation-method changes, foundation redesign, delay), Metro green-infrastructure stormwater requirements even on single-family permits, Belle Meade's 8% hardscape cap, Forest Hills impervious-surface caps, and Oak Hill's steep-slope and tree-canopy review. Price them per deal, not per model.

From list to contract — the playbook for these three areas

1. Work the stack top-down
Start with the 3 four-signal and 14 three-signal properties. Before any outreach, run the three per-property checks the bulk data can't do: Clerk & Master delinquent status (next newspaper ad Oct 30, 2026, ahead of the Nov 18 sale), Nashville Ledger foreclosure notices, and the assessor record card's appraisal history.
2. Verify HPR per parcel before you pitch a split
The HPR flag on this page is a size/zoning proxy only — and the legal rule is narrower than the flag: two-family HPR is allowed only in Metro R districts (never RS, which is single-family only), on a lot meeting the district minimum, with both units together inside the parcel's coverage cap and each detached unit no taller than 1.5× its width. The model already restricts the two-unit exit to R-district lots ≥ 15,000 sq ft (443 of the 5,699 modeled; 64 survive the offer screen). Confirm zoning, overlays, and lot configuration on the Metro Parcel Viewer for the specific parcel — an overlay can block both the split and the demolition itself.
3. Trace only the top — Tracerfy
Skip tracing stays gated exactly as in your Houston system: Tracerfy at ~2¢/hit, only on properties already ranked by the stack (2+ signals first), never the whole 3,174-property list. Run your 5-owner accuracy-test discipline on Nashville owners before any bulk trace. Free first pass: the assessor record itself — owner name, mailing address, and sale history often identify an estate, trust, or LLC whose registered agent is findable in Tennessee Secretary of State business records at $0.
4. Contract Tennessee-shaped — SB 909
Tennessee's first wholesaling statute (Public Chapter 72, effective March 25, 2025; Tenn. Code Ann. §§66-4-401–403) keeps assignment legal with: written disclosure to the seller, in bold, large-font print in the agreement, that you're acquiring an equitable interest and intend to market/assign it; disclosure of the interest's nature to the end buyer; and written notice to the seller at least 3 business days before an assignment takes effect. Market your contract, never the house. Confirm the enrolled text with a Tennessee attorney before contracting.
5. Sell to the builders already on the map
The blue permit layer is your dispo radar: the applicants pulling demolition and new-construction permits in 37215 and 37205 this year are the buyers already paying for lots in these exact streets. No builder publishes a teardown buy-box — acquisition runs through relationships, and the permit record tells you whose door to knock on.

The list itself applies three exclusions to the 8,677 screen-pass properties: the owner exclusion — 2,929 removed (2,824 owned for under 5 years, 416 owned by non-individuals, overlap counted once); trusts and estates of named individuals count as individuals and stay — the MLS exclusion: 49 properties currently listed for sale were removed, matched by address against Redfin (RealTracs) Active + Coming Soon listings pulled Oct 28, 2026; the match file refreshes weekly in the daily job (the MLS picture is as of that pull — a property that went under contract after it would still show until the next match) — and the offer-acceptance exclusion: 2,525 removed where the modeled contract price (per-property new-build exit from the nearest verified new-build sales − that property's planned build size × its jurisdiction's build cost per sq ft − a $20,000 demolition allowance − 8% soft costs − 15% builder profit − the $15,000 target assignment fee) was not above the owner's last recorded sale price, or fell below 90% of the county total appraisal (land + improvement appraisals combined); properties with no recorded sale price were judged on the appraisal test only (2,520 failed the appraisal test, 731 failed the last-sale test, 726 failed both). (2) Tax delinquent, pre-foreclosure, and high tax increase have no bulk public source in Davidson County and appear only as per-property checks — no property on this page is shown as verified for them. (3) Probate shows 0 estate-titled parcels in this pull; the obituary-verification pass on the 30 longest-tenure individual owners is merged where it found evidence — 4 owners confirmed deceased and still on title, named in the signal section; three remain on the list and are flagged on their detail cards, while the fourth (2234 Castleman Dr) was removed by the offer-acceptance screen described above; owners not yet checked show “obituary check pending.” (4) The HPR flag is a lot-size/zoning proxy, not confirmed eligibility; the two-unit exit in the model is restricted to Metro R districts on lots ≥ 15,000 sq ft, because RS districts are single-family only. (4a) The planned-size and build-cost model (v2) rests on: Metro Code Table 17.12.020A coverage percentages (Metro has no floor-area cap — the cap is two stories over the coverage limit, garage inside the footprint); Belle Meade Ordinance 2024-14 FAR; Forest Hills Ordinance 2024-03 FAR range — the parcel's Forest Hills district is not in county data, so those lots are modeled at a 0.13 central FAR; and Oak Hill's Zone C standard (0.18 FAR, 10,000 sq ft cap), which rests on a single secondary summary of the city's posted standards and is the model's weakest input — verify with the City of Oak Hill before underwriting an Oak Hill deal. Contextual front setbacks (in Green Hills routinely 50–100+ ft) and overlays can bind below the coverage cap on individual lots. Construction costs are published-source ranges, not quotes: the luxury band $325–$425/sq ft comes from NAHB cost data, Nashville builder guides, and one Brentwood-area lender package (~$333/sq ft all-in); RSMeans/Marshall & Swift figures are paywalled and were not obtained. Permit-stated construction costs in county records understate true hard costs and are used here only as a floor check.
